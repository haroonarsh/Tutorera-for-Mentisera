# How to Choose an Online Tutor: A Practical Guide for Parents and Students

**Status:** Draft — requires editorial review, citations to current awarding-body materials where relevant, and publication approval.

**Suggested URL:** /blog/how-to-choose-online-tutor

Choosing an online tutor is easier when you compare evidence rather than promises. The best fit depends on the learner's curriculum, current understanding, preferred teaching approach, available time and budget. A high rating or advanced qualification alone does not establish that a tutor will teach a particular student effectively.

## Start with one specific learning goal

Before searching, write down the student's year or grade, examination board where relevant, subject, recent difficulties and target. “Improve maths” is too broad to guide a first lesson. “Solve simultaneous equations independently and explain each step” gives a tutor something observable to assess. For examination preparation, check the syllabus version and whether the learner needs content review, practice with unfamiliar questions, time management or feedback on written answers.

## Check curriculum experience, not just subject labels

A tutor who teaches general mathematics may not be familiar with a student's exact GCSE, IGCSE, O Level, A Level or AP course. Ask which specifications they have taught, how they select practice material and how they distinguish knowledge gaps from exam-technique issues. If a tutor says they can teach every curriculum, request a concrete example of adapting the same topic for different levels. Confirm qualifications and experience using the evidence actually available; do not assume a platform profile guarantees teaching outcomes.

## Ask how the first lesson will work

A useful first session usually includes a short diagnostic task, a worked explanation, student practice and a clear next step. Ask: What will you assess? How will you know whether I understand? What should I do between lessons? How will you explain a concept differently if the first explanation does not work? A tutor should be able to answer without promising a particular grade.

## Check the online teaching setup

For calculation-heavy subjects, ask whether the tutor uses a shared whiteboard or another way to show steps clearly. For languages, ask how speaking and written feedback are handled. Test audio, connectivity, device compatibility and screen sharing before committing to a recurring schedule. Students should have a quiet space, the required books and a reliable way to submit work. If internet access is unstable, agree on a sensible rescheduling policy.

## Compare total cost and scheduling

Compare the advertised lesson rate, duration, currency, cancellation conditions, platform charges and any package commitment. The lowest price is not necessarily the best value; neither does a high rate guarantee results. A useful comparison is the total cost of a realistic four-week plan alongside the amount of feedback and preparation included. Avoid making payments outside approved channels where platform protections depend on recorded transactions.

## Consider safeguarding and communication

For children and teenagers, a parent or responsible adult should understand who the tutor is, how sessions are arranged and which communication channels are used. Discuss appropriate lesson boundaries, privacy, recordings and what to do if a student feels uncomfortable. Do not share unnecessary identity documents or personal contact details. Follow the platform's current safety and reporting policies.

## Review progress after several sessions

Choose two or three measurable indicators: independent completion of a target task, fewer recurring mistakes, quality of explanations or performance on a short practice set. Ask the tutor for specific examples rather than a vague claim of improvement. If the student remains confused, change the plan or reconsider the match.

## A short interview checklist

Ask prospective tutors: Which curriculum and level have you taught? What will you assess in the first session? Can you demonstrate a typical explanation? How will you assign and mark practice? How will you report progress? What equipment do we need? What is the complete cost and cancellation policy? How do you handle student safety?

## Final decision

Shortlist tutors whose experience fits the learning goal, compare transparent terms and use an initial lesson to test teaching clarity. The right tutor should help the learner become more independent over time, not more dependent on paid sessions.
